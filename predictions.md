2. **Loss is directional.** When an attribute changes, the new value matches the occupation's one-shot prior in over 70% of changes (pilot → lighter-skinned and male, nurse → female, social worker → darker-skinned and female).
3. **Loss is asymmetric.** Counter-stereotypical seeds lose the stereotyped attribute at least twice as fast (per-step hazard) as stereotypical seeds; the stereotypical seeds' losses are mostly self-healing (the same value returns within 3 steps).
4. **Where it happens.** Under Photo, most losses are captioner omissions (the caption stops naming the attribute before the image changes). 5. **The ratchet.** Recovery of a lost counter-stereotypical attribute is rare (under 10% of losses); survival curves are close to geometric.
6. **Prestige.** The share of losses that move toward lighter skin and male presentation is highest for pilot and lowest for social worker.
7. **Skin tone drifts toward SDXL's one-shot distribution and homogenises:** within-occupation standard deviation of L* at step 50 is lower than at step 0 for every occupation.

Operational definition of a **theme attractor** (the "convincing model"; the analog of the persistent atlas, same dwell and visit rules): each story is coded, by a judge outside the loop and with names and places ignored, into a fixed schema of categorical fields (lead age band, lead gender, lead role, setting place, setting time, conflict type, ending valence, death present, reconciliation present, nature central) plus one theme sentence; the theme sentence is embedded (MiniLM) and the categorical fields are read as tuples. A theme attractor in a cell is (i) a persistent atlas cluster of theme-sentence embeddings with ≥ 3 persistent visits (dwell ≥ 5) from ≥ 2 trajectories, or (ii) a tuple of (age band × conflict type × ending valence) that ≥ 3 of 20 trajectories hold for ≥ 5 consecutive steps. Collapse = the effective number of tuples (exp of Shannon entropy) at steps 50–99 falling below its value at steps 1–10. Nulls: the step-1–10 distribution, and a within-trajectory step shuffle (no step trend by construction). Reliability: a second judge on 300 stories, Cohen's kappa per field; fields under 0.6 are reported but excluded from the verdict; Brad hand-codes 50.

**E3-A, anonymised re-embedding (existing data, no generation).** Names and places in every E3-1 summary and in every story (all six cells) are replaced by a neutral token (the gate's name filter: capitalised non-initial tokens with a rare lower-case form) and re-embedded with MiniLM; nearest other-trajectory distance (steps ≥ 25), share within 0.15, Vendi at step 99 and the persistent atlas are recomputed.
- A1. Specifics are not the barrier: the median nearest other-trajectory distance falls by less than 0.05 in every cell (summaries 0.44–0.52 → ≥ 0.40; stories 0.37–0.56 → ≥ 0.33), and no persistent attractor shared by ≥ 2 trajectories appears at radius 0.15 in any E3-1 cell. Decision rule: a fall ≥ 0.10 or any shared attractor → names and places were the barrier, and the anonymised embedding atlas becomes the primary text measure; otherwise the barrier is the plot itself, and the theme layer (E3-B) is where an attractor can live.

The arithmetic behind speculation 1: if a model has W equally likely wells and every trajectory ends in one, the chance that a given pair of trajectories shares a well is about 1/W whatever n is, so the number of sharing pairs grows with the number of pairs: 190 at n = 20, 4,950 at n = 100. The **pair-sharing rate** is therefore the primary measure: the share of unordered trajectory pairs that both make a persistent visit (dwell ≥ 5, radius 0.15, hysteresis 1.25) to the same persistent cluster of the story embeddings (a cluster with ≥ 3 persistent visits in all: the gate's shared-cluster rule, read pairwise; wording clarified 2026-11-02 while both runs were in progress and before any result was read); computed raw and anonymised (E3-A's procedure); k scales with the data so that the points per centroid stay as in the 20-trajectory cells (k 40 per 2,000 trajectory-steps, so k 200 for the 100-trajectory cell on its own), and, as a check that the procedure is not doing the work, on random 20-trajectory subsamples of the 100 at k 40 (the sampling distribution the n = 20 cell is one draw from).
