Three follow-ups to the E3 gate (`reports/e3.md`, 2026-10-29: no shared attractors across trajectories in any text cell). A = anonymised re-embedding of the six existing cells; B = theme-layer coding of the same 12,000 stories by a judge outside the loop; C = new cells, the rewrite loop with an absolute length ("length about 500 words") under claude-haiku-5-5, and the two new models. All five C cells are finished (Haiku 2026-11-01; Mistral and Qwen E3-2b overnight, queue done 2026-11-02 07:17 PST). D (the theme-bottleneck loop) has a prediction and no run; it waits for Brad.

**Gate: A1 fails as written, B1–B6 fail, B7 holds; C1 holds for Haiku only (the 7B models still shrink under an absolute target: Qwen 83 → 286 words, Mistral 308), C2 holds in all three E3-2b cells on everything but one two-trajectory cluster under Haiku, C3 holds on five of seven clauses and fails on the two that say Mistral has no shared attractor (it has one, the first in any text cell without anonymising), C4 holds. The three results agree on one picture. Names and places are about two-thirds of what keeps trajectories apart in MiniLM space, and once they are removed one or two plot-level clusters shared by two or three trajectories appear in every compress–expand cell, none under the matched null. But the theme layer does not collapse: the effective number of (age × conflict × ending) tuples does not fall under Sonnet and falls only mildly under the 7B models, no theme-sentence cluster is shared at the gate's radius, and each trajectory's theme stays traceable to its own seed for as long as its story does (theme-space half-life 40 / 11 / 18 steps for Sonnet / Qwen / LLaVA against 45 / 14 / 22 in story space). What is strong is the marginal grain, the model's default values field by field: a woman lead in 63–77% of late stories under the Claude models, a hopeful ending in 64–93% everywhere, present day, internal or community conflict, and under Sonnet's compress–expand loop a death in the story rising from 29% of early steps to 51% of late ones. Haiku adds the size axis: shorter seed memory (half-life 11 vs Sonnet's 45), a narrower name pool (top-10 cover 0.54 vs 0.40, "Maya" leading 200 of ~1,000 late stories), the same grain as Sonnet (modal tuple young_adult × internal × hopeful, two of three fields shared; woman 70%, internal conflict 52%), and a rewrite loop that is nearly the identity (631 verbatim copies in 1,980 steps; Sonnet made 2).**

What the fields say. The per-field late-window modes (`theme.md`, second table) are the grain: woman 0.63 (Sonnet E3-1), 0.71 (E3-2), 0.68 (Qwen E3-1), 0.46 (LLaVA); hopeful 0.64–0.93; present_day where coded; internal conflict under Sonnet (0.43), social_or_community under Qwen (0.39); parent_or_grandparent the modal secondary character under Sonnet E3-1; death present rising 0.29 → 0.51 under Sonnet E3-1 and only there. These are the defaults the loop exposes, and they are model-level; they are not points the trajectories move toward together, because the effective number of tuples never falls and no two trajectories share a theme cluster.

Post-hoc checks (added after the verdicts were read; labelled so in `theme.md`):
- *lead_age is the weak field.* Haiku codes 'unclear' 0.60 of the subsample, Sonnet 0.38 (κ 0.62), so the predicted tuple is dominated by 'unclear' in the 7B cells. Tuple B (gender × conflict × ending; κ 0.90 / 0.79 / 0.63) tells the same story: ratio 1.25 Sonnet (null 1.05), 0.78 Qwen (null 1.06), 1.08 LLaVA; modal 0.15 / 0.27 / 0.11.
- *Judge noise is not the reason the tuples move.* Haiku test-retest on 287 stories: κ 0.78–0.94 by field (lead_age 0.86, conflict 0.88, ending 0.88). So the step-1 drop in own-tuple hold (0.65 of trajectories keep their coded age across the first compress–expand) is real: the categorical fields are labile from the first step while the gist is sticky.
- *The gist is the seed's.* Seed identification by theme sentence (own step-0 theme nearest among the 20): half-life 40 (Sonnet), 11 (Qwen), 18 (LLaVA), still at 0.41 / 0.15 / 0.35 at step 99; rewrite cells never fall below 0.95. The codebook draft (k-means k 20 over 240 theme sentences) groups by seed across models, not by model: the harbour-and-fog seed, the online-persona seed and the grieving-boy seed each form their own cluster with members from all three models at steps 1–95. A codebook built this way would be a seed list. The closed-codebook pass is therefore not run; the next useful codebook would come from late-window sentences only, or from the D loop.

## Deviations and notes
- D16 applies to Haiku (temperature rejected; `temperature_applied: false` in every record). The Haiku configs carry the Ollama `digest_prefix`/`host` keys from the template; the backend is `anthropic` and they are unused.
- `story_meta.flags.refusal` fires on dialogue as in D19 (368 in e3_1_haiku, 100 in e3_2b_haiku; all calls `end_turn`); not used.
- E3-C is reported by a new script rather than by `e3_report.py`, whose cells and verdicts are hard-coded to the six gate cells; the atlas in `c.md` is a joint k-means over the five C cells only, so cluster counts are not pooled with the gate's. The cluster sheets from the two-cell pass were replaced by the five-cell ones.
- Mistral's step-0 stories are its own (no shared step-0 with Qwen or Haiku), so cross-model comparisons of C are across different step-0 texts from the same seeds, as between the gate's models.
- Post-hoc analyses (tuple B, test-retest, theme-space memory) are labelled as such in `theme.md` and here; no prediction was edited.

## What this means for D
The question D asks, whether destroying the specifics every step (theme sentence as the bottleneck) produces the cross-seed convergence the 50-word summary did not, is sharper after A–C: names were most of the barrier (A), the theme layer does not converge on its own (B), and the seed's gist survives a 50-word summary for 11–40 steps (B post-hoc). D1 as written (≥ 3 shared attractors under Haiku, half-life ≤ 10, nn-other ≤ 0.30) is the direct test, and the judge's theme sentences from B are a free preview of its input distribution. Held for Brad's call.

Judge outputs (the raw input to `theme.md` and `c.md`, one JSON line per story with the API request id and token usage) are kept gzipped in `reports/e3/judge/`; the analysis scripts read the uncompressed copies in `analysis/cache/<run>/` (git-ignored).
