## What is strong in the draft

- The seed-as-reference survival design. It needs no population baseline and turns the question into a measurable per-step quantity.
- Counter-stereotypical and stereotypical seeds for the same occupation, so asymmetry is a within-design comparison.
- Separating captioner omission from generator override. This is the mechanistic payoff and it is exactly what our per-step records support.
- The one-shot neutral baseline as the generator's own prior.

## Insights and suggested changes

1. **The mechanism is a ratchet, and that should be the stated hypothesis.** An attribute can be lost two ways per step (the captioner omits it, or the caption names it and the generator overrides it) but it is regained only by chance from the generator's prior. For a stereotype-matching seed, loss is self-healing: the prior restores the same attribute. For a counter-stereotypical seed, one omission is permanent. So asymmetric survival is nearly guaranteed by construction; the interesting numbers are the per-step hazards (omission and override, by seed group) and whether the loop's end state is more skewed than the one-shot prior. The gravity machinery (per-step hazard, Kaplan-Meier survival, stationary distribution) transfers directly.

2. **Define amplification against the one-shot prior, not against the seed.** Seed retention measures forgetting; forgetting alone would make the loop end at the generator's one-shot distribution for that occupation. Amplification means the loop ends beyond it. Test: endpoint demographic distribution of loop trajectories (all seed groups pooled, and neutral seeds alone) vs the 100-image one-shot distribution for the same neutral prompt. If equal, the loop launders bias (erases the seed and reverts to the prior); if more skewed, iteration multiplies it. Both are publishable, and only the second is what "feedback loops amplify" claims.

3. **Person dropout is the dominant process and will starve the measurement.** With Hintze's instruction, half the people are gone by step 25. E6's photo instruction may slow this but will not stop it; the attribute-tracking variant will help more. Treat dropout as a competing risk (a trajectory that loses its person is censored for attribute loss, not counted as a flip) and report person survival alongside attribute survival. Fifty steps is enough; most of the action is in the first 20.

4. **Seed framing must make the attributes visible.** "In her spacesuit" hides gender and skin tone behind a helmet; firefighter gear, pilot sunglasses and nurse masks do the same. Use "close-up portrait, helmet off / face visible" framings, and check the step-0 images before launching the run.

5. **Captioner must not be the image coder.** The captioner is inside the loop; coding the images with it would be the CLIP mistake again. Caption-side scoring can use the caption text (plus an LLM check); image-side coding needs an independent model (Claude or FairFace), validated on ~300 human-labelled images, stratified by group so per-group error rates are estimable. For analysis, bin Monk tone into three bands; ten-point coding of rendered skin under stylised lighting will not be reliable.

6. **The neutral-seed loop is the most valuable control.** Ghosh and Caliskan (2023) found SD's default "person" is a light-skinned Western man. Running the neutral prompts through the loop asks whether the loop's attractor for "a nurse" or "a person" is even narrower than one-shot. That is the direct test of the feedback-loop suggestion.

7. **Cost is small.** 10 occupations x 2 genders x 3 tones (+ age) x 4 trajectories x 50 steps is about 12,000 steps, 8-9 h and about $20 on the pod at A0 speed; the one-shot baseline (10 x 100 images) is under an hour. Three instruction variants fit under $100. Claude Sonnet as captioner adds API cost (about 12,000 image calls per variant).

8. **Housekeeping.** E5 and §5 say `llava:13b`; A0 and all runs so far use `llava:latest` (7B, D2). "A0-photo" and "A0-lit" are referenced in E5 and E6 but defined nowhere; the A0-plain arm discussed on 2026-10-20 ("Describe this image. The description must be 50 words or fewer…") is presumably A0-photo and should be defined in §3 or §4 with its own prediction. E6's "photo" instruction should be that exact string so A0-plain doubles as E6's control.

## Literature to anchor the write-up (verify citations before use)

One-shot bias in text-to-image: Bianchi et al. 2023 (FAccT), Luccioni et al. 2023 (Stable Bias). Caliskan's group: Wolfe & Caliskan 2022 (markedness: "American == White"), Ghosh & Caliskan 2023 ("Person" == light-skinned Western man; sexualisation of women of colour in SD). Bias amplification as a concept: Zhao et al. 2017. Feedback loops: Taori & Hashimoto 2023 (data feedback loops amplify dataset bias), Wyllie, Shumailov & Papernot 2024 (fairness feedback loops when training on synthetic data). Those loops are training-time; E6 is an inference-time loop with frozen models, which is the gap.

## Suggested order

A0-plain (A0-photo) next on the pod after E1-cap, since E6 needs its instruction as the control. Then E6 pilot: one occupation pair (astronaut / nurse), all attribute cells, 4 trajectories, 50 steps, photo instruction, plus the one-shot baseline; code it by hand before building the automated coder.
